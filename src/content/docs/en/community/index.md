## How can we effectively grow a language on Common Voice? {#grow-a-language}

Creating opportunities for a diversity of people to contribute to Common Voice ensures the dataset serves as many people as possible. We’ve created resources and templates that you can use!

### Events {#events}

You can run events to help people contribute. It’s easier than you think. You could do it online with a videoconferencing tool, or in person if it’s safe. Check out our templates and resources for running events.

Planning an event for more than 1,000 people? Tell the team at least a month ahead: [commonvoice@mozilla.com](mailto:commonvoice@mozilla.com).

#### Ask Mozilla to share your events {#share-event}

Have a previous or upcoming event? Share it with us and we will announce it to our community or write a blog about it!

### Social media {#social-media}

You could use social media platforms to get the message out. Share posts that explain why it matters, and get in touch with other people talking about issues like language rights, voice AI, or bias in tech. See more advice on running a social campaign, including content you can re-use.

### Partnerships and networks {#partnerships}

Find others who care. That could be universities, language schools, advocacy groups or data science communities. Reach out and explain clearly how they can help and why. See our template outreach emails.

**Get creative!** Your language community will be unique, and these are just a few ways to get started. Please consider adding your community channels to the [Language communities](language-communities.md) page.

## Community Facilitators {#facilitators}

Community Facilitators help a language grow on Common Voice: they welcome new contributors, run events and keep in touch with the Common Voice team. Signed-in users can apply on the Common Voice site; the team reads every application and replies to the contact address you give.

**What we ask:** your title, full name and how to address you, how to reach you (email, and Matrix or Discord if you use them), the languages you prefer for correspondence, your timezone and a good time for an optional call, the languages you want to support, and anything else you want to tell us.

**Language Community Experts:** you can also apply as a Language Community Expert, if you have expert-level or working linguistic knowledge of a language and can advise its community. Tell us your languages and level, your familiarity with speech technology, and any professional or institutional affiliation.

Approved Community Facilitators can apply for honorarium funding, usually for a community event: see [Honorarium funding](#honorarium).

## Honorarium funding {#honorarium}

Only approved Community Facilitators can apply for honorarium funding, usually for a community event; the team checks this when it reads your application. Applications are ranked on long-term impact, contributor development and sustained engagement beyond a single event. Languages not yet on Common Voice are especially welcome.

**What we ask:** a contact email, the languages, the title of the event, what the event is, and the amount you request with its currency (the programme usually funds up to 300 USD for an event). You can also tell us how it builds local capacity, the planned dates, its campaign or community page, the outcomes you expect, how you will report the results, your ideas for a lasting community, and whether you want the event announced on Common Voice. The rest of the conversation continues by email.
